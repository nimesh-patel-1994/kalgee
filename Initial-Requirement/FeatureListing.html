Multi-Tenant Inventory Management Platform: Feature Listing

Version: 1.5 (adds the ERP Mobile App, Section 14; v1.4 was Release 1/2 naming, technology, Flutter customer app in Release 2) | Date: 11 Nov 2026 | Status: Ready for freeze

This document lists every feature planned for the platform. Once it is frozen, the Technical Document will be written. Features I suggested beyond the original brief have been accepted and are now regular requirements (listed with details in Section 13). Items marked (#) are design notes rather than user-visible features (see Section 11). Items marked (###) are future expansions that are not built now. Every item without a tag is part of Release 1 (first release). Items tagged Release 2 are in scope but planned after the first release. The release tags on accepted suggestions and Compliance items are confirmed. Items marked Compliance come from Indian regulations found in research (see Section 12) and must be confirmed with your CA or legal advisor. Everything else comes directly from the brief or your answers.

1. Product Overview

Two products in one platform:

  1. Admin Panel: one shared web panel used by the Super Admin, Company Admins and Company Staff to run inventory, purchasing, sales, expenses, marketing and exhibitions.
  2. eCommerce Website (one per company): a separate customer-facing store for each company, powered by APIs exposed by the platform. Each store shows that company's products only.

Tenancy model: 1 Super Admin, many Companies, exactly 1 Admin per Company, many Staff per Company. All company data is fully isolated.

Scale assumption: about 10 companies at launch, with 50 to 60 products per company.

Two Flutter mobile apps (separate apps):

  • ERP Mobile App (Section 14): for Company Admins and Company Staff, built after the web system is complete (Phase 6 of the plan). It carries the day-to-day features of the Admin Panel that suit a phone, with the same APIs, rules and permissions as the web panel. It is not a copy of the whole panel.
  • Customer mobile app (Release 2): for each company's eCommerce store, using the same storefront APIs (see 5.14).

Technology: .NET for the backend and APIs, React for the Admin Panel and the storefront website, PostgreSQL as the database; Flutter for both mobile apps.

4.18 Production Batches, Quality Check and Batch Pricing

Terms: an inventory lot is raw material received or entered as opening stock, with its own quantity, price and expiry date (for example 1000 kg). A batch is one production run made from one lot, with its own batch number, manufacturing date, expiry date, output, cost and selling price. The raw material lot and the batch each have their own expiry date.

Flow: Raw material lot → Batch created → QA In Progress → QA Passed (to Packing), QA Failed (Discard) or Rework (back to QA In Progress) → Batch-wise priced stock → Website and bills.

  1. Opening raw material: the company starts with raw material lots (quantity, price, supplier, expiry). One lot can produce many batches.
  2. Batch creation: choose product, lot and raw material quantity used; the quantity is deducted from the lot. A batch draws from one lot only. The next lot is used only after the earlier lot is fully used (oldest expiry first), so every batch has one clear expiry and price basis. If the lot has less left than planned, the last batch is simply smaller. Lots are never mixed in one batch, because mixing older and newer material would change the expiry date.
  3. Output and loss: enter the actual output. The system shows loss quantity and loss % (100 kg in, 95 kg out = 5% loss), with an alert if loss exceeds a threshold set per product. Batch expiry is entered by the user (default from product shelf life), with a warning if it is later than the lot's expiry.
  4. Quality Assurance (QA): a batch is always in one of four states: QA In Progress, QA Passed, QA Failed or Rework. Rework sends the batch back to QA In Progress, and the final result is then QA Passed or QA Failed. Every batch gets a QA report: date, checked by, parameters (custom per product, such as taste, colour, moisture, weight, appearance) with result and remarks, overall Pass/Fail, and attachments (lab report, photos). A batch cannot be packed or sold until QA passes.
    • Fail: the batch is discarded with a reason. Its raw material cost is booked as a loss in profit reports.
    • Pass: the batch goes to packing.
    • Rework: the batch is reworked (reason, actions taken, extra material and labour cost added to the batch cost, extra loss recorded) and returns to QA In Progress. The number of reworks is recorded.
  5. Packing: the passed batch is packed into sellable units (pack size and count). Packing and label stock is deducted here, and packing loss is recorded. Packed units become sellable stock under that batch. Every pack label carries the batch number, manufacturing date and expiry date printed from the system, and each pack is traceable to its batch.
  6. Batch costing: batch cost = raw material at lot price + packing + labels + labour + share of production overhead (electricity, rent, salaries, misc). Cost per unit and margin are shown for every batch. Daily figures are approximate: overhead is estimated from the month's recurring amounts spread per day. Month-end costing: after the month closes, the Admin runs "Finalise month costing", which spreads the actual rent, electricity, salaries and misc across that month's batches and updates batch cost and profit. Reports show Approximate until the month is finalised and Final afterwards.
  7. Batch-wise pricing: each batch has its own selling price and MRP (defaulting from the product, editable at packing). Sales bills and exhibition issues use batches oldest-expiry first.
  8. Website roll-out: the website shows the price of the batch currently on sale. When that batch sells out, the next batch goes live with its new price and new expiry date. An order takes only what remains of the current batch. Admin can pause a batch from the website Release 1.
  9. Batch status: Created, QA In Progress, Rework, QA Passed, QA Failed (then Discarded), Packing, Ready for sale, Sold out.

Example: Lot of 1000 kg. Batch 1 uses 100 kg and yields 95 kg (5% loss); QC fails, so it is discarded. Batch 2 uses 50 kg and yields 47 kg (6% loss); QC passes, so it goes to packing. The lot has 850 kg left for later batches.

4.19 Staff, Salary and Payroll

  • Payroll on/off: if salary is handled by the CA or outside the platform, the Company Admin switches Payroll off in Master Configuration and enters the monthly salary total as an expense instead. Salary still shows in profit reports.
  • Two staff types: Permanent staff are paid monthly (fixed salary); Temporary staff are paid per day or per hour. Both can be in the same company.
  • Staff profile: name, designation, staff type, joining date, pay basis (monthly, per day, per hour), pay rate, allowances, deductions and statutory details (PAN, PF/UAN, ESI number, state for professional tax). A staff member may or may not have a login.
  • Daily attendance (present, half-day, absent, leave, overtime) with monthly summary; hours in and out for hourly staff.
  • Monthly payroll run by the Company Admin: salary from attendance, plus allowances, bonus and overtime, minus advances and deductions; approve, then mark paid with date and payment mode.
  • Statutory deductions (#): PF, ESI, Professional Tax and TDS on salary are calculated automatically and shown on the slip, with employer contributions tracked separately. Each staff member has on/off flags for PF and ESI. Rates, wage limits and slabs (Professional Tax is state-wise) live in a configurable statutory master so they can be updated when the law changes. TDS uses the tax regime and declarations the staff member provides. Monthly PF, ESI, PT and TDS statements are available for the CA. Filing and paying them on government portals is planned for Release 3.
  • Salary slip per staff per month (PDF) with earnings, deductions, net pay and attendance. Staff see and download only their own slips; the Admin sees all.
  • Bank details: staff add and update their own (account holder, account number, IFSC, bank, branch, UPI). Details are checked through a third-party bank verification API (IFSC and account details) and can be saved only after the check passes, so no Admin approval is needed. Numbers are masked and every change is logged.
  • Salaries post automatically as an expense and feed batch cost and profit reports.
  • Advance and loan tracking with a deduction schedule Release 2.
  • Payroll reports: salary register and month-wise payout.

4.20 Inventory Control Enhancements

Found in leading inventory software (Zoho Inventory, Odoo, Fishbowl) and not yet covered above. - Physical stock count: full and cycle counts by category, location or batch; enter or scan counted quantity; variance report; stock is adjusted only after Admin approval. - Write-off types: expired, damaged, sample, gift, own use, QC-rejected; each with a reason and booked to the right expense. - Approval rules: adjustments and write-offs above a set value need Company Admin approval; bulk adjustments through Excel. - Purchase order and goods receipt (GRN): receive goods against a PO, partial receipts, ordered vs received vs billed comparison, supplier returns. Low-stock alerts convert to draft POs in one click. - Incoming raw material QC: each raw material lot is accepted or rejected on receipt, with remarks, before it becomes usable stock Release 2. - Batch recall: mark a batch as recalled; it is blocked from sale and from the website, the customers and bills that received it are listed, and they can be notified by message. - Barcode scanning (phone camera or USB scanner) in billing, exhibition billing, stock count and goods receipt. - Combo/bundle products (gift packs made of several products; stock deducted from the parts) Release 2. - Customer-group price lists (retail, wholesale, distributor) applied automatically on billing Release 2. - Multiple locations (factory, shop, stall) with stock transfers, in-transit status and bin/rack labels Release 2. - Cash book, bank book and day book with cheque tracking Release 2.

4.21 Product Compliance and GST Rules (India) Compliance Release 1

Legal Metrology (packaged goods) - Fields per product and pack: manufacturer/packer/importer name and address, generic name, net quantity, MRP inclusive of all taxes, consumer care details, country of origin (imported goods), dimensions where relevant, date of manufacture or packing, best before or expiry date (taken from the batch). - MRP is stored per batch (see 4.18) and always tax-inclusive; a selling price above MRP is blocked. - Pack labels can be printed with these fields.

FSSAI (applies to categories with the Food compliance profile) - Company FSSAI licence number; per product: veg/non-veg symbol, ingredients, nutrition information per 100 g/ml, allergen information, storage and usage instructions, batch/lot number on the label. - Shown on the product page and label template.

GST rules - HSN is required on every product: it is a mandatory field when a product is added and in Excel imports, and it is printed on all GST invoices, including B2B and export bills. Minimum 4 digits. From ₹5 crore turnover, 6 digits are required: at ₹4.5 crore the Admin gets a popup to prepare the 6-digit codes, and at ₹5 crore a product without a 6-digit HSN cannot be billed. Because every product already has HSN, activating Export (a future expansion (###)) needs no separate HSN step. - Turnover tracker (follows the GST "aggregate turnover" definition): the financial year's sales across all channels, including taxable, exempt and export sales, without GST and cess. The Admin can add turnover brought forward from earlier years. Each company has one GSTIN and one PAN, so no other registrations are combined. At ₹4.5 crore the Admin gets a popup to prepare 6-digit HSN codes. On crossing ₹5 crore, a second popup explains that B2B and export e-invoicing (IRN) generally starts from the next financial year and stays applicable even if turnover later falls; B2C invoices are outside e-invoicing. - e-Invoicing applicable setting with an "applicable from" date, as in Tally. Once set, the e-invoice fields are used on B2B invoices; the IRN module itself is Release 2 (see below). - GSTIN format check for company, customers and suppliers; state code and place of supply derived automatically. - Bill of Supply for composition-scheme or exempt sales; reverse-charge flag on purchase bills. Release 2 - Invoice numbers are consecutive, unique per financial year, and no longer than 16 characters. - Delivery challan for stock sent to exhibitions or on approval without a sale; the invoice follows when a sale happens. - e-Invoice (IRN and QR code) is mandatory for B2B invoices once a company's turnover reaches ₹5 crore. Not in the first release, but invoice fields (IRN, acknowledgement number, QR, transporter and vehicle details for e-way bill) are reserved so it can be added as an optional module Release 2. - CA pack tables follow GSTR-1 sections: B2B, B2C, credit/debit notes, HSN summary, documents issued (an export table comes with Export (###)).

14. ERP Mobile App (Flutter) Mobile, Phase 6

14.1 Purpose and principle

  • A separate Flutter app for Company Admins and Company Staff (Android first, then iOS). The Super Admin and the CA use the web only.
  • It brings the features of the web Admin Panel that people need while moving (stall, shop, warehouse, factory floor, owner away from the desk). It is not a copy of the whole panel: heavy set-up, analysis and rare tasks stay on the web (14.6).
  • Same APIs, same business rules, same roles and permissions, and the same module structure and names as the web panel. A person who knows the web panel finds the same modules, fields and statuses on the phone. No separate backend.
  • Built in the last phase, after the web system is complete and accepted, using the developer's Flutter boilerplate (Clean Pattern Boilerplate).

14.4 Mobile-ready APIs (applies to the web APIs from Phase 1)

  • Versioned APIs (/v1), with the OpenAPI document as the contract used to generate the Flutter client; no breaking change without a new version.
  • Pagination and small payloads on every list (cursor or page and size, field selection where useful), so lists load fast on mobile data.
  • Device-aware sign-in: refresh-token rotation per device, a device name on each session, remote logout of one device, and registration of a push token per device.
  • Idempotent creates for invoices, goods receipts, expenses and adjustments, using a client-generated request key, so a retried or offline-synced request never creates a duplicate.
  • Offline sync endpoints for billing and scans: upload a queue of invoices and scans with client ids and times; the server returns per-item results (accepted, rejected with reason, conflict). Stock is checked on the server when the queue syncs; the no-negative-stock rule and FEFO still apply.
  • Attachments (receipt and delivery photos) uploaded with size limits and compression guidance.
  • Server-time and clock safety: the server stamps the final time; the device time is stored as the "created on device" time.
  • Push notification service (device tokens, topics per company and role, delivery log).
  • Tenant isolation and permissions are enforced by the API exactly as for the web; the app never decides access alone.

14.5 Offline rules (billing and stock scans only)

  • Offline works only for: scanning items and viewing stock cached on the phone, creating sales invoices, and exhibition stall billing. Every other module needs a connection and says so clearly.
  • The app keeps a local queue; the user sees how many items are waiting and the sync result for each one.
  • Invoice numbers are reserved so offline invoices never clash: each device gets its own number range per company, or a temporary number that becomes final on sync (decided in the technical document; the GST numbering rules in 4.21 must still hold).
  • Cached stock is shown with the time it was last updated. If a synced invoice would make stock negative, the item is returned to the user as a conflict to fix; it is never silently dropped.
  • Offline data on the device is encrypted and removed on logout.

14.6 Web only (not in the mobile app)

Company profile and Master Configuration, tax and GST set-up, the CA filing pack and CA review, roles and permission design, staff creation, bulk import and export, subscription and billing pages, all Super Admin screens, store content, blogs, policies and FAQs, payroll runs, marketing campaign set-up, and the full report library and profit analytics. Users who need them open the web panel.

14.7 Mobile non-functional requirements

  • Android and iOS from one Flutter code base; phones first, tablets work but are not specially designed.
  • Camera barcode scanning only (no Bluetooth printers or scanners). Printing and sharing use the phone's share and print features.
  • Secure storage for tokens, automatic logout on inactivity (same default as the web), app lock, screen protection for sensitive screens, rooted-device warning.
  • The same design language and terms as the web panel (colours, names of modules and statuses).
  • Cold start in about 3 seconds on a mid-range phone; list screens show the first page in about 2 seconds on a normal mobile connection.
  • English first; the app is built so more languages can be added (multi-language UI is Release 2 on the web too).
  • Store publishing is done by the developer (Play Store and App Store); the toolkit builds and tests but never publishes.

14.8 Later mobile additions (not in the first mobile release)

Read-only report library with share, marketing campaign send, payroll view, purchase order creation, Bluetooth printers, tablet layouts, and the CA read-only login.
